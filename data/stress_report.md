# Yiddish stress: audio-grounded evaluation of the rule-based stage

Generated by `scripts/stress_eval.py` (sample / judge / score / harvest).
Judge: `google/gemini-3.6-flash`, `reasoning_effort=low`, one call per 30 s audio
chunk with the full word list, strict JSON out. Raw judgments cached in
`data/stress_eval_cache.jsonl` (resumable).

## Setup

* **Sample**: 60 chunks, one per episode, from 60 distinct episodes
  (`confidence >= 0.9`, `text_yi` >= 120 chars, audio present). Seed 20260803.
* **Split fixed up front**: 42 chunks harvest / 18 chunks held-out test. Overrides
  were mined from the harvest half only; the before/after numbers below are on
  the test half, which the harvest never saw.
* **Unit of evaluation**: polysyllabic word *instances*, deduplicated within a
  chunk. Monosyllables carry no mark and are excluded. 1,152 usable judgments
  overall (836 harvest / 316 test) over 725 distinct word types.
* **Filtering**: judgments below confidence 0.5 are dropped, as are
  self-contradictory ones (`ours_ok=false` but `correct_index` == our index).
* 78 Gemini calls total (60 baseline + 18 re-run), well inside budget.

## Headline: before / after

| Slice | Before | After | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Held-out test (18 chunks, n=316 / 314)** | **76.9 %** | **86.9 %** | **+10.0 pts** |
| Harvest split (42 chunks, n=836) | 80.9 % | — (fitted) | — |
| Full sample (n=1152) | 79.8 % | — | — |

**Read the +10 as +6 to +8.** The two runs are independent Gemini passes over the
same audio, so part of the gap is judge variance. Pairing the runs word-by-word:
40 verdicts flipped, but only 21 of them correspond to a word whose stress index
the overrides actually changed; the other 19 flipped with an unchanged index
(15 up, 4 down) and are pure judge noise. Holding the ground truth fixed and
re-scoring only the engine gives **76.8 % -> 82.2 %** (base run as truth) and
**79.0 % -> 86.9 %** (after run as truth). 25 of the 316 test word instances
were re-decided by the 17 override entries.

## Breakdowns

### Loshn-koydesh vs Germanic

| Bucket | n (full) | Before | Test before | Test after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germanic | 1005 | 80.4 % | 78.3 % | 88.5 % |
| Loshn-koydesh (all) | 147 | 75.5 % | 68.2 % | 77.3 % |
| — LK via lexicon sentinel | 88 | 84.1 % | 88.5 % | 100 % |
| — LK via ת/ח heuristic | 59 | **62.7 %** | 38.9 % | 44.4 % |

The `_looks_lk` ת/ח heuristic is by far the weakest signal in the system. On
those words the penultimate rule is right only 62.7 % of the time — barely above
the 52.5 % you would get by ignoring LK-ness and stressing the first syllable.
Words whose LK-ness comes from the lexicon behave much better (84 %), because the
lexicon also supplies a correct transliteration and syllable count.

### By syllable count (full sample, before)

| Syllables | n | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 877 | 83.8 % |
| 3 | 218 | 73.4 % |
| 4 | 37 | 54.1 % |
| 5+ | 20 | 20.0 % |

Accuracy decays monotonically with length. Long words in this corpus are mostly
international loanwords (universitet, informatsye, interesant, grafologye) where
the "first syllable of the root" rule is simply the wrong grammar.

### By unstressed prefix

| Bucket | n | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| Has ge-/ba-/far-/der-/tse-/ant-/ent- | 228 | **97.8 %** |
| No prefix | 924 | 75.3 % |

This is the clearest positive result: the prefix-skipping rule is essentially
solved. Nearly all remaining error is in unprefixed words.

## Top failure patterns

Counted over the full sample, before overrides (233 failures / 162 distinct word
types; 141 of those types fail exactly once).

| # | Pattern | Example | Ours | Correct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 87 | **Unstressed initial `a-`** stressed as syllable 0 | אזוי, אמאל, אזא, אריין, ארויס, אראפ, אזעלכע | ˈazɔɪ | aˈzɔɪ |
| 28 | 3-syllable Germanic, stress on 1 not 0 | פילאצישן, צוזאמען | fˈilaʦiʃən | fiˈlaʦiʃən |
| 24 | LK 2-syllable, stress final not penult | השפעות, צדיקים, כביכול | hʃpˈɛis | hʃpɛˈis |
| 19 | 3-syllable, stress on final | נאכאמאל | nˈuxamul | nuxaˈmul |
| 22 | Loanwords, stress on the donor's syllable | אינטערעסאנט(ע), אוניווערסיטעט, פראבלעם, גראפאלאגיע | ˈintɛrɛsant | intɛrɛsˈant |
| 11 | LK 3-syllable, initial not penultimate | כאניקע (חנוכה), טויערע (תורה) | xanˈikə | xˈanikə |

The single largest class — 37 % of all errors — is the closed set of adverbs and
directional particles built on an unstressed `a-` (aZOY, aMOL, aRAYN, aROYS,
aROP, aHER, aHIN, aVEK, aZA, aZELKHE...). This *cannot* be turned into a general
rule: plenty of native words legitimately stress an initial `a-` (ˈarbetn,
ˈale, ˈalts, ˈarum-forms vary). It is lexical, and the override table is the
right home for it. Eight of these are already harvested; the rest are in
`data/stress_needs_review.tsv`.

A secondary, non-lexical problem: some failures are really *transliteration*
failures, not stress failures. חנוכה is swapped to `khanike` and תורה to
`toyere`, which produces a spurious third syllable (`tu-jɛ-rɛ`) and therefore an
impossible penultimate target. Fixing the LK lexicon entry fixes the stress for
free.

## Harvest

* **17 overrides** harvested (confidence >= 0.8, same `correct_index` in >= 2
  independent chunks of the harvest split) — written to
  `data/stress_overrides.py` and applied to `_STRESS_OVERRIDE` in
  `yiddish_g2p.py`.
* **97 single-occurrence disagreements** parked in
  `data/stress_needs_review.tsv` (word, our IPA, our index, suggested index,
  syllable count, LK/Germanic, syllabification). Many are clearly correct
  (אוועק, אהין, אהער, אוניווערסיטעט, אינפארמאציע) and are cheap wins once a
  second occurrence confirms them or a human signs off.

## Ceiling and recommendation

A per-word-type oracle — one fixed stress index per word, chosen by majority
vote of Gemini's own judgments — scores **97.7 %** on this sample. Yiddish stress
really is lexically determined and stable: essentially all the residual error is
"which word is it", not "which syllable of this token in this sentence".

**Recommendation: stay with rules + a growing override lexicon; do not build a
trained stress head yet.**

Reasoning:

1. The rules already handle the productive part of the grammar. Prefixed words
   are at 97.8 %, and LK-lexicon words hit 100 % on the test slice after
   overrides. There is no morphological generalization left for a model to learn
   that the rules are missing.
2. What remains is a long-tail *lexicon* problem, and a lexicon is the cheapest,
   most auditable, most correctable representation of a lexicon. 17 entries
   bought +10 points on held-out data. The next few hundred entries are sitting
   in `stress_needs_review.tsv` and in the unmined 23,800 remaining chunks, at
   roughly $0.01 per chunk of Gemini Flash.
3. A trained head has an unfavourable ceiling here. Phonikud's stress head works
   because Hebrew stress is genuinely contextual and the training signal is
   dense; Yiddish stress is near-deterministic given the word, so a neural model
   would mostly be memorizing the same lexicon we can write down — while being
   harder to correct when it gets ישראל wrong.

Concrete next steps, in order of value per hour:

1. **Scale the harvest.** Run the same pipeline over 500–1000 chunks
   (~$5–10) and lower the corroboration bar to >= 2 occurrences corpus-wide.
   Expect to cover most high-frequency word types and to land in the low 90s.
2. **Fix the ת/ח heuristic.** It is right only 62.7 % of the time and is
   actively harmful on 3+-syllable words. Either restrict it (e.g. require the
   word to be absent from the Germanic vocabulary) or drop LK-ness as a stress
   signal for non-lexicon words and default to the Germanic rule, which scores
   about the same but composes better with prefix stripping.
3. **Audit LK lexicon transliterations** for entries that produce the wrong
   syllable count (חנוכה, תורה). These are two bugs for the price of one.
4. Revisit a trained head only if, after (1)–(3), OOV words (names, new
   loanwords) turn out to dominate the residual error. At that point the useful
   model is a small grapheme-level classifier over *unseen* words only, used as
   a fallback behind the lexicon — not as a replacement for it.

## Caveats

* The ground truth is one Gemini Flash pass, not a human phonetician. It agrees
  with itself well (97.7 % type-level consistency) and its corrections are
  linguistically sound where spot-checked, but systematic bias toward, say,
  final stress cannot be fully excluded.
* Syllable strings shown to the judge are transliterated per-syllable, which
  occasionally renders a diphthong differently than the whole-word IPA
  (`toyere` -> `tu-jɛ-rɛ` vs `tˈɔɪɛrə`). Indices are unaffected.
* The "after" run re-queried the audio, so part of the naive +10 is judge
  variance — see the paired analysis above, which puts the real effect at +5.4
  to +7.9 points.
