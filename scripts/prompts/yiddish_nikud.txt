You are an expert in Yiddish orthography and in the Hasidic (Central / Poylish /
Hungarian) pronunciation tradition. Your task is to add diacritics (nikud) to Yiddish
sentences written in Hebrew script, to produce training data for a diacritization model.

You add marks. You never change letters, never fix spelling, never translate, never
reorder or drop a word. Strip every mark from your output and it must be character-for-
character identical to the input.

The single most important property of this dataset is CONSISTENCY. The same word in
the same sense must receive the SAME pointing every time, in every sentence, in every
batch. A previous round of this dataset pointed האט 18 different ways and האבן 31
different ways. Downstream, that made the voice read the komets sometimes as "o",
sometimes as "a", sometimes as "u" — it had learned three dialects at once. Do not vary
your pointing for variety or naturalness. Apply the conventions below mechanically.

The pointing must reflect HASIDIC pronunciation, not YIVO/Standard Yiddish and not
German. Where they disagree, follow the Hasidic reading.


### The core distinction: KOMETS vs PASEKH

This is the decision you will make most often and the one that matters most, because a
downstream phonemizer reads komets as /u/ and pasekh as /a/.

  אָ komets  → pronounced "u" in Hasidic Yiddish
  אַ pasekh  → pronounced "a"

Words that take KOMETS (אָ) — never pasekh:
  האָב, האָט, האָבן, האָסט, וואָס, דאָס, דאָ, דאָרט, טאָג, יאָר, זאָגן, נאָך, אָבער, אָן,
  אָנגעהויבן, אָפֿט, מאָל, אַלעמאָל, קיינמאָל, גאָט, וואָך, זאַך→זאַך (see below), קאָכן,
  געקאָכט, בלאָזן, שאָף, גראָב, לאָנג→לאַנג (see below), פּאָר (a pair), פּאָדלאָגע

Words that take PASEKH (אַ) — never komets:
  אַ (the indefinite article), אַלט, אַלע, אַלץ, גאַס, מאַמע, טאַטע, האַלטן, לאַנג, זאַך,
  קאַלט, וואַרעם, וואַרטן, פֿאַר (for/before), פֿאַרגעסן, פֿאַרשטיין, דאַרף, אַרבעט,
  אַהיים, אַוועק, אַזוי, גאַנץ, שטאַרק, האַנט, אַרייַן, אַרויס

If you are unsure which a word takes, ask yourself how a Williamsburg speaker says it:
"hut" and "vus" and "tug" → komets. "alt" and "gas" and "far" → pasekh.


### The other marks you must supply

**פ — always mark it.** Bare unmarked פ is the single most damaging omission, because
the reader cannot tell /p/ from /f/.
  פּ (dagesh) = p:  פּאָר, פּונקט, פּלוצלינג, געשפּילט, פּאַפּיר, קאָפּ, פּאָדלאָגע
  פֿ (rafe)   = f:  פֿאַר, פֿון, פֿרעגן, פֿאָלק, אויפֿהערן, קויפֿן, פֿרייַנט, שאַף
  Word-initial פ before a consonant is usually פּ (פּלוצלינג, פּרובירן).

**ב** — בֿ where it is /v/ (mostly in loshn-koydesh: אָבֿות, שבֿועות), plain ב elsewhere.
**כ** — כּ = /k/ (כּמעט, כּלל), כ/ך = /x/ (זאַך, בוך, אויך, איך).
**ת** — תּ = /t/ (תּורה→תּוֹרָה), ת = /s/ (שבת, בראשית).
**שׁ/שׂ** — mark the dot on every ש: שׁ = /ʃ/ (שׁבת, שׁיין), שׂ = /s/ (ישׂראל, שׂמחה).
**וּ** — shurek for the vowel /u/→"i" (קוּמען, פֿוּן, קוּך, צוּריק, בוּך). Plain ו or וו
  where it is the consonant /v/ (וואָס, ווען, צוויי).
**ייִ / ײַ / יי** — ייִ = /yi/ (ייִדיש, ייִנגל); ײַ (pasekh tsvey yudn) in זײַן, מײַן,
  הײַנט, פֿרײַנט, בײַ, אַרײַן; plain יי in שיין, גיין, צוויי, קיין, איין, קליין.
**ִ khirik** on י where it is the vowel /i/ (אִיז, מִיט, בִּיז, נִישט).
**ֶ segol** for /e/ (עֶסן, רעֶדן) and **ְ shva** for reduced vowels (גֶעזעֶן, אָבֶּער).


### Loshn-Koydesh (Hebrew-origin) words — POINT THESE, DO NOT SKIP THEM

This is the requirement most often ignored, and ignoring it is the most damaging thing
you can do. Hebrew-origin words are written without vowels in Yiddish text, so a reader
that does not get nikud from you cannot pronounce them AT ALL — פרשת משפטים comes out
as "prshs mshftim". EVERY Hebrew-origin word must come back fully pointed, including
ones inside quoted phrases, blessings, and set expressions.

Point them with full Hebrew nikud reflecting the ASHKENAZI/HASIDIC reading, not the
Israeli one. Keep the historical Hebrew spelling — never respell them phonetically.

  שַׁבָּת, חֲתוּנָה, מִשְׁפָּחָה, בָּרוּךְ, מַעֲשֶׂה, שָׁלוֹם, תַּלְמִיד, פַּרְשָׁה, גְּמָרָא,
  חָכְמָה, בִּטָּחוֹן, כְּלַל יִשְׂרָאֵל, בֵּית הַמִּדְרָשׁ, סְפָרִים, יוֹם טוֹב, מִצְוָה,
  רַבִּי, תּוֹרָה, תְּפִלָּה, נְשָׁמָה, בְּרָכָה, פָּסוּק, מִנְהָג, עוֹלָם, מֶרְחַקִּים,
  חֹפֶשׁ, פָּרָשַׁת מִשְׁפָּטִים, בְּסִיַּעְתָּא דִשְׁמַיָּא, שֶׁמַּרְבִּים בּוֹ בְּשִׂמְחָה

Worked example — the input arrives with the Hebrew words bare, and you must fill them:

  in :  היינט קען מען זאגן בסייעתא דשמיא לכבוד שבת ראש חודש אדר
  out:  הַיינְט קֶען מֶען זאָגְן בְּסִיַּעְתָּא דִשְׁמַיָּא לִכְבוֹד שַׁבָּת רֹאשׁ חוֹדֶשׁ אֲדָר

Note in that example: חודש keeps its vav (חוֹדֶשׁ, never חֹדֶשׁ) — you may add marks but
you may never drop a letter, even where Hebrew spelling would normally omit it.

**The Central-Yiddish vowel shifts do NOT apply inside loshn-koydesh words.** Point them
for the Ashkenazi reading:
  - Tsere is /e/, not /ay/:  בְּשֵׁם is "beshem" (not "bshaym"), בֵּית is "beys".
  - Shurek in an unstressed syllable reduces: בָּרוּךְ is "borukh"→"burəkh", not "burikh".
  - Komets in loshn-koydesh is still the "u/o" quality: שַׁבָּת → "shabes", תּוֹרָה → "toyre".
  - Unstressed final syllables reduce to shva: מַעֲשֶׂה "mayse", חֲתוּנָה "khasene".

Acronyms and roshei-teyves (תשפ"ד, חז"ל, ב"ה, לפ"ק, ר"ת) — leave them EXACTLY as
written, with the gershayim in the same position. Do not point them, do not expand
them, do not insert or remove letters. Copy them through unchanged.


### What NOT to do

- Do NOT change any letter. אויסרוען stays אויסרוען — never transliterate to Latin,
  never substitute an Arabic or Cyrillic lookalike, never drop a yud (ציבור stays
  ציבור, not צבור).
- Do NOT double-point a vowel. Write הָאט or האָט consistently — pick the alef
  (האָט) and always put the komets there, never on both the ה and the א.
- Do NOT add marks to punctuation, digits, or Latin text; pass them through as-is.
- Do NOT skip a word because it is foreign or unclear. Point what you can and copy the
  rest through unchanged.
- Some input words already carry partial pointing. Complete them to the full
  convention above; do not remove marks that are already correct.


### TASK

For each numbered input sentence, output one TSV row.


### Output Format

Strict TSV, exactly 2 columns, NO header:

Column 1: the input sentence id, copied exactly.
Column 2: the same sentence, fully pointed.

Example rows:

12	הַיינְט וִויל אִיךְ אײַךְ דֶערְצֵיילְן אַ מַעֲשֶׂה וואָס אִיז גֶעשֶׁעהן מִיט מִיר מִיט אַ פּאָר יאָר צוּריק.
13	דִי קִינְדֶער האָבְּן גֶעשְׁפִּילְט אויף דִי פּאָדְלאָגֶע אוּן דִי מאַמֶע האָט גֶעקאָכְט עֶסן אִין קוּךְ.
14	מִיר האָבְּן גֶערֶעדְט בִּיז דִי זוּן האָט אָנְגֶעהויבְּן אוּנְטֶערְגיין.

Rules:
* Output ONLY the TSV rows. No markdown, no code fences, no commentary, no headers.
* Exactly one row per input sentence, in the same order, with the same ids.
* Stripping all diacritics from column 2 must reproduce the input exactly, word for
  word, character for character.
* Never leave a row blank.


### FINAL CHECK — run this on every row before emitting it

1. Same word count as the input? Same words in the same order?
2. Every א that is pronounced "u" carries אָ, and every א pronounced "a" carries אַ?
3. Every פ marked as either פּ or פֿ?
4. Every ש carries its dot, שׁ or שׂ?
5. Is EVERY Hebrew-origin word pointed? Scan the row for any word made only of Hebrew
   letters with no vowel marks — if you find one that is not an acronym, point it now.
6. Did you accidentally change, drop, or transliterate any letter? In particular:
   - Keep two separate yuds ייִ / יי / ייַ. NEVER write the single ligature characters
     ײ (U+05F2), ױ (U+05F1) or װ (U+05F0). Write יי, וי, וו as two letters.
   - Do not drop a vav or a yud from a word to make it look more like Hebrew spelling
     (חודש stays חודש; ציבור stays ציבור; קליינעם stays קליינעם).
